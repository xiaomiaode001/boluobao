# Controlled Error System

The style depends on construction mistakes, not surface noise. Errors must look like consequences of drawing quickly by hand: the pen was lifted, the page was rotated, a shape was judged by eye, or color was added after the ink.

## Spontaneity levels

- **Restrained:** use 2–3 error families, each visible once or twice. Suitable for products, portraits, and content with strong identity locks.
- **Loose (default):** use 4–6 error families across the page. Keep important silhouettes readable while letting secondary details drift.
- **Wild:** use 6–8 error families, larger proportion shifts, cropped marks, and bolder near-collisions. Use only when requested; never sacrifice subject identity or exact text.

## Error families

Choose a varied subset. Do not apply every family to every object.

1. **Stroke restart:** one selected long contour stops and resumes slightly off-axis, or receives one short corrective echo line. The event stays local; it does not repeat around every edge or across the full perimeter.
2. **Open contour:** a small gap remains at a low-information edge; diagnostic boundaries stay closed enough to read.
3. **Pressure jump:** one contour thickens abruptly or fades where the pen direction changes.
4. **Registration miss:** pencil fill crosses the ink by a small amount or stops early, leaving paper between fill and contour.
5. **Coverage patch:** color density changes within one shape; repeated strokes reveal direction and hand pressure.
6. **Ellipse slip:** bowl rims, plates, wheels, or windows are slightly lopsided and not perfectly concentric.
7. **Perspective disagreement:** one secondary plane tilts differently, one table edge leans, or distant objects are only loosely scaled.
8. **Proportion shorthand:** a handle, limb, garnish, chair, or awning is slightly too large or too small.
9. **Selective omission:** remove nonessential symmetry, texture, repeated windows, or ingredients after the subject becomes recognizable.
10. **Overlap confusion:** one harmless tangent or near-touch occurs between a note, arrow, shadow, or secondary object.
11. **Baseline drift:** short handwritten labels tilt or climb; letter size changes within the phrase while remaining readable.
12. **Page accident:** one faint smudge, dry-marker stop, erased ghost, cropped doodle, or offset shadow appears locally.

## Semantic safety zones

Construction errors may change how a real edge was drawn; they must not create a new semantic-looking object or mark.

- **Safe zones:** low-information contour edges, secondary texture, color-fill boundaries, shadow edges, background paper, and abbreviated repeated detail.
- **Protected zones:** the hero silhouette, food surfaces, the front face of a vessel, identity-critical geometry, faces, exact text, and any clean area that could be mistaken for a label or logo.
- A stroke on the hero must correspond to a real contour, seam, fold, ingredient, reflection, or explicitly requested decoration.
- Do not place isolated dark blocks, floating strokes, emblem-like marks, pseudo-characters, accidental calligraphy, or invented hardware on protected zones.
- A page accident must read immediately as a faint physical event such as a smudge, erased ghost, spill, or dry-marker stop. If it could be read as writing, a symbol, a logo, or an extra object part, remove it.

When moving an error away from a protected zone, preserve its variety by placing it on a harmless contour restart, fill edge, shadow boundary, or background mark instead.

## Default loose recipe

Use this as a starting distribution, not a quota:

- place 2–4 restart events across the whole page, each on a different selected long contour;
- use only 1–2 short corrective echo lines; each stays local and covers less than roughly 15% of its source contour;
- leave 2–5 small registration misses across the page;
- distort 1–2 ellipses or secondary planes;
- omit 2–4 repeated low-value details;
- create one spacing tension or harmless tangent;
- add at most one faint page accident.

Keep the hero silhouette, face, vessel front, logo-free diagnostic features, exact text, and event order as clean anchors. If the result looks incompetent rather than alive, reduce error size before reducing error variety.

Most short and medium lines remain continuous. Restart spacing must never become periodic, and a contour must never read as stitched, perforated, beaded, or constructed from repeated hash marks. Construction error is localized evidence of hand movement, not a page-wide ink texture.

## Prompt language that works

Prefer causal phrases such as:

- drawn directly in ink without an underdrawing
- corrected once by hand, with the first line still faintly visible
- colored quickly after the line drawing, leaving small misses and spillovers
- perspective judged by eye rather than constructed with a ruler
- secondary details abbreviated when the page became crowded

Avoid vague requests such as `messy`, `bad drawing`, `low quality`, `random scribble`, or `add noise`. They often damage readability without producing authentic spontaneity.

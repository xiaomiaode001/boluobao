# Style DNA

## Core impression

A warm, handmade travel notebook: observational rather than polished, graphic rather than realistic, dense with personal notes yet easy to scan. The charm comes from confident simplification, physical-media grain, and small human irregularities.

## Material and surface

- Base: uncoated warm ivory paper, approximately `#F1E9DC` to `#E9DFCF`.
- Line medium: nearly black soft pen or brush pen, approximately `#332A25`; occasionally dark brown rather than absolute black.
- Fill medium: colored pencil, wax crayon, or dry felt marker. Coverage is translucent and streaky; paper tooth remains visible.
- Shadow: one muted lavender-gray, dusty rose, or warm brown patch offset beneath an object. Shadows are graphic stains, not physically accurate lighting.
- Finish: matte, analog, slightly faded. No glossy highlights, bloom, plastic surfaces, or digitally perfect gradients.

## Line system

- Outer contours are assertive and 1.5–2.5 times heavier than interior marks.
- Lines vary in pressure, wobble, and closure. Corners round off; repeated strokes sometimes overshoot.
- Interior detail uses sparse hatching, short dashes, dots, or occasional short double contours.
- Imperfection should read as intentional hand movement, not low-resolution noise.
- Most silhouette segments remain confidently continuous. On a few long edges only, leave one tiny gap, restart once, or place one short corrective stroke beside the first.
- Avoid a uniform outline brush. Adjacent objects may look as if they were drawn with different pressure or after the pen was lifted.

### Line rhythm and hierarchy

- Treat continuity as the default and interruption as an event. A long contour receives at most one restart or correction in a local region; the echo line should cover less than roughly 15% of that contour.
- Keep the silhouette and vessel-defining edges visibly darker and heavier than folds, ingredient separations, hatching, and material texture. Interior marks should recede at first glance.
- Never build an edge from evenly spaced dashes, beads, hash marks, or repeated micro-gaps. Never trace a second line around the full perimeter. Paper tooth and colored-pencil grain create texture; the ink line itself must not become a uniform dotted or stitched texture.
- Use interior ink only for diagnostic structure. On repeated foods, draw a representative subset of pleats, kernels, noodle strands, seeds, layers, or garnish; let colored-pencil blocks carry the remaining material information.
- On bowls, plates, cups, and steamers, draw only structural rings that explain rim, opening, wall, or base. Omit redundant concentric tracings and decorative bands that do not clarify construction.

## Shape and perspective

- Reduce every subject to a strong silhouette and a few diagnostic cues.
- Food and vessels: slight top-down view, flattened ellipses, visible rim, simplified ingredient clusters.
- Clothing and objects: mostly frontal catalog-like view with minimal perspective.
- Characters: flat, elongated or compact bodies, oversized head features, simple limbs, blocky dark hair, deadpan or exaggerated expressions.
- Depth uses overlap and one cast-shadow patch; avoid complex vanishing points.
- Preserve a few constructive mistakes: lopsided ellipses, a rim that is not perfectly parallel to the bowl base, slightly leaning verticals, or a secondary object that is a little too large. These slips must not obscure identity or function.

## Color language

Use 4–7 colors per page, biased toward earthy, sun-faded hues:

- tomato red `#C85643`
- burnt orange `#D47B3B`
- mustard `#D8AB3B`
- olive `#6E7B3D`
- dusty teal `#5F7F81`
- slate blue `#71839A`
- plum `#76506D`
- faded rose `#C66D79`

Black, paper, and one accent color may dominate. Let fills escape or miss the contour by a tiny amount. Avoid neon, large areas of saturated digital color, and mathematically even color blocking.

Color registration is intentionally imperfect. Some small zones should remain unfilled; some fills should cross the ink edge; neighboring regions need not share the same coverage density. Never turn this into a uniform halo or global blur.

## Page composition

- Default portrait page with generous paper visible around clusters.
- Build an irregular two-column or scattered-grid rhythm. Align by eye, not by ruler.
- Alternate large hero sketches with small icons, arrows, speech bubbles, ribbons, prices, or short comments.
- Text and image should interlock: notes tuck beside silhouettes, wrap around bowls, or point toward details.
- Dense pages still need 8–15% quiet space. Do not fill every gap with decoration.
- A clean single-subject output may omit the notebook-page layout while preserving paper, line, fill, and shadow behavior.
- Keep one mildly awkward relationship: a note almost touching an object, one unusually wide gap, a cropped doodle, or a cluster that sits off-center. Balance the page by eye without correcting every tension.

## Typography and annotation

- Handwritten lettering has varied baseline, size, spacing, and stroke pressure.
- Headings may sit in a yellow ribbon, rough box, speech bubble, or underlined phrase.
- Body notes are compact and conversational; use arrows and tiny emphasis marks.
- Exact text is a content lock. Never substitute copied writing from style references.
- For image models, prefer a few short labels. Reserve long passages for post-generation typesetting.

## Signature motifs

Use sparingly and only when content supports them:

- tiny deadpan avatar or reaction face
- curved arrow with a short side note
- soft colored-pencil cast shadow
- banner/ribbon heading
- simple speech bubble
- date, ordinal, small price, rating, heart, or star
- dashed travel path or compact timeline band

## Anti-style constraints

Avoid photorealism, anime polish, vector-clean geometry, watercolor washes, oil paint, smooth cel shading, 3D rendering, scrapbook stickers, faux-vintage filters, ruled notebook lines, perfect typography, heavy beige grunge, and overloaded kawaii decoration. The result should feel drawn in one person's notebook, not assembled from clip art.

Also avoid commercial sticker polish: consistently thick contours, fully closed shapes, perfectly centered subjects, identical shadow treatment, uniformly filled color, or decorative wobble added as an even filter.
